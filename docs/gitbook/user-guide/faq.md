# FAQ

## Does PaCo send any data to the internet?

No. Rules, usage and requests stay on your Macs and travel only over your home network, encrypted.

## Does the block work on other networks, like a friend's Wi‑Fi?

Yes. The block is applied on the child's Mac itself, on every network. Local devices on whatever network the Mac is on stay reachable.

## Does it still work when my Mac is away?

Yes. The child's Mac keeps enforcing the last rules it received. Requests and Parent Access need your Mac to be connected.

## Can my child get around it?

Not from a standard account through PaCo itself. The rules are stored in a protected system location and enforced by a system service. A child with an administrator account, or with physical access to restart into macOS Recovery, can disable it. A proxy server running on another device in your home network could also bypass the block, because local devices stay reachable.

## Does a VPN get around the block?

No. A VPN needs the internet to connect, and that is blocked.

## Does PaCo filter websites?

Not yet. PaCo turns internet access on or off as a whole. Content filtering is on the roadmap.

## Can I use PaCo on a single, shared Mac?

Yes. Set it up as **Parent**. The rules of the active profile apply to that Mac, for **every account on it, including yours**. Use the manual switch on **Home** when you need the internet during a blocked period.

## Can one child's Mac have two parents?

No. A child's Mac is paired with one parent Mac at a time.

## Can I manage several children?

Yes, with one profile each, and each child's Mac paired separately. In this version every child's Mac enforces the rules of all profiles; see [Child profiles](profiles.md).
